## Goals

- Choose an array to solve a real-world problem and justify (orally) the choice based on the constraints of the situation.
- Create multiple arrays for a given number of objects.

## Lesson Purpose

The purpose of this lesson is for students to use what they know about arrays to decide how to arrange chairs in a way that makes the most sense for a school concert.

## Narrative

This lesson is optional because it does not address any new mathematical content standards. This lesson does provide students with an opportunity to apply precursor skills of mathematical modeling (MP4).

In previous lessons, students learned that an array is an arrangement of objects into rows with an equal number of objects in each row. They determined how many objects were in an array and represented the number as a sum of equal addends. In this lesson, students use what they know about arrays to show different ways chairs could be arranged for a school band concert. Students may represent the arrays and the total number of chairs in any way that makes sense to them. They then think about which array would be the best for this situation and make a recommendation for how the seats should be arranged.

## Standards

Building On 2.OA.C.4

The purpose of this activity is for students to consider some of the different arrays they represented in the previous activity in the context of the band concert. Students think about reasons why one array might be better than another for this situation. Students determine which array they think would be best for arranging the chairs for the concert and write about their recommendation. When students think about the real-world implications of their model, they model with mathematics (MP4).

## Launch

- Groups of 2
- Display the following descriptions of arrays:
- 1 row, 24 columns
- 3 rows, 8 columns
- 6 rows, 4 columns
- 'Here are some options for how the chairs can be arranged for the band concert. What do you notice about these arrays?' (One only has one row and it is really long. One has a lot of rows and the others have a lot of columns. Two of them are more alike than the third.)
- 'What are some things the teacher should think about to help decide how to arrange the chairs?' (how wide the stage is, how easy it will be to see each student from the audience, how many of each instrument there are and where they should be sitting)

## Activity

- 'Decide which arrangement of chairs you would recommend for the band concert. Represent that arrangement and give reasons why you think this is the best arrangement.'
- 7 minutes: independent work time
- 3 minutes: partner discussion
- Monitor for students who chose different arrangements, or the same arrangement for different reasons.
