## Goals

- Create (orally and in writing) examples and nonexamples of collections of objects that could be represented by a given number.
- Explain (orally) estimation strategies.

## Lesson Purpose

The purpose of this lesson is for students to use what they know about place value to estimate what given numbers could represent in a school.

## Narrative

This lesson is optional because it does not address any new mathematical content standards. This lesson does provide students with an opportunity to apply precursor skills of mathematical modeling (MP4).

In previous lessons, students were introduced to a hundred as a unit made up of 10 tens. They also learned that the three digits in a three-digit number represent hundreds, tens, and ones. In this lesson, students consider the magnitude of different numbers and decide what those numbers might represent in their school.

## Standards

Addressing 2.NBT.A.1

The purpose of this activity is for students to think about the quantity of different items in their own classroom. Students begin by sharing different items they think these numbers could not represent. They then make a list of items they think each number could represent. They discuss what work would need to be done to determine if any of their ideas are correct. If time permits, students collect this data and determine what the numbers represent. If students are not able to collect this data, the teacher can reveal what the numbers represent in their classroom. When students collect data or explain how it could be collected, they model with mathematics (MP4).

## Launch

- Groups of 2
- Display the previously made chart with numbers of classroom items.
- 'I have done some research and found two numbers that represent the exact quantities of two items in our classroom.'

## Activity

- 'Record the numbers on the table in your student book. Then think about the items in our classroom. Record as many items as you can that the numbers could represent, and items that the numbers could not represent.'
- 5 minutes: partner work time
- Share and record responses, starting with items the numbers could not represent.
- 'Now we have a list of items these numbers could represent. What could we do to see if any of the items on our list are correct?' (We could count each of the items and see how many there are. We could put them into groups of 10 or 100 and see how many groups we have so we know if we are close. If we are, we could count them to get an exact number.)
- Group students based on the item they think is represented by the number. Have each group work together to find out if the given number matches the quantity of that item.
- 5 minutes: small group work time

## Activity Synthesis

- 'What did your group learn about the quantity of the item you chose?' (We learned that there are way more \_\_\_\_\_ than we thought. When we counted to 200 and saw that there were still a lot more, we stopped because we knew there were more of this item than the given number. We learned that the given number represented the item we counted.)

'Today we estimated quantities in order to determine items that might be represented by given numbers.
