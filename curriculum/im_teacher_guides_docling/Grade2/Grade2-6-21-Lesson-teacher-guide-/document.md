## Goals

- Ask (orally) questions that encourage comparing two collections of coins, and compare the values of coin collections.

## Lesson Purpose

The purpose of this lesson is for students to compare the value of coin collections and to work with shapes.

## Narrative

This lesson is optional because it is an opportunity for extra practice that not all classes may need. In Activity 1, students learn Stage 1 of the Would You Rather? center. In this stage of the new center, students practice identifying coins and comparing the values of coin collections. In Activity 2, students choose to continue working on Would You Rather? or choose between two previously introduced centers focused on shapes.

## Launch

- Groups of 2
- Give each group a spinner and a copy of the directions.
- 'We are going to learn a new center called Would You Rather? We will compare the values of coin collections. Let's play a round together.'
- 'One partner spins to get a group of coins. They identify the different coins in the collection and count how many of each. Then they ask a question that compares the amount they spun to a different group of coins that they make up.'
- Demonstrate by spinning the spinner and asking a question. Provide example questions that show changing the coins but keeping the number of coins the same, or keeping the coins the same but changing the number of each coin in the collection. For example:
- 'Would you rather have 3 quarters or 3 pennies?'
- 'Would you rather have 4 nickels and 6 dimes, or 4 dimes and 6 nickels?'
- 'Would you rather have 1 quarter, 1 dime, and 1 nickel, or no quarters, 1 dime, and 7 nickels?'
- Invite the class to discuss and share which group they would rather have.

- 8 minutes: partner work time
