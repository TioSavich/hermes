## Goals

- Explain (orally) how to draw a given shape by describing its attributes.
- Identify (orally) a shape, based on given attributes. Ask questions to elicit the attributes needed to identify the shape.

## Lesson Purpose

The purpose of this lesson is for students to practice describing, naming, and drawing triangles, quadrilaterals, and hexagons, based on their defining attributes.

## Narrative

This lesson is optional because it is an opportunity for extra practice that not all classes may need. In Activity 1, students learn Stage 3 of the Which One? center, which was first introduced in kindergarten. In this new stage, called Which One?-Grade 2 Shapes , students work with triangles, quadrilaterals and hexagons as they ask their partner 'yes or no?' questions to figure out what shape they chose. In Activity 2, students learn Stage 2 of the Can You Draw It? center, which was first introduced in grade 1. In this new stage, also called Can You Draw It?-Grade 2 Shapes, students continue to work with triangles, quadrilaterals, and hexagons as they describe and draw two-dimensional shapes.

## Standards

Addressing 2.G.A.1

The purpose of this activity is for students to learn Stage 2 of the Can You Draw It? center. Students describe and draw two-dimensional shapes. One student picks a shape card, without showing their partner. They describe the shape to their partner, who draws it based on the description. Together they compare the shape that was drawn to the shape on the card. If the student has drawn the shape correctly, they keep the card. The partner with the most cards at the end of the game wins. Students work with triangles, quadrilaterals, and hexagons. Before playing, remove any cards that do not show a triangle, a quadrilateral, or a hexagon.
