## Goals

- Explain (orally) strategies for adding within 100.

## Lesson Purpose

The purpose of this lesson is for students to practice addition and subtraction within 100 that does not require composing or decomposing a ten.

## Narrative

This lesson is optional because it is an opportunity for extra practice that not all classes may need. Students learn new stages of centers that were introduced in grade 1. In the first activity, students learn Stage 3 of the Capture Squares center. In this new stage, called Add within 20, students find the value of sums within 20. In the second activity, students learn Stage 6 of the Five in a Row center. In this stage, students practice finding the value of sums within 100 that require composing when adding by place.

![Image](document_artifacts/image_000020_e3e2e022c37b0c2ef8940ab8f88789c08aeab85179e7827af71f40d191a28383.png)

## Standards

Addressing

2.OA.B.2

The purpose of this activity is for students to learn Stage 3 of the Capture Squares center. Capture Squares was introduced in IM Grade 1. In this stage, students spin to get a number (6-10) and flip a card (0-10) and find the value of the sum. The spinner includes a wild space where students can choose their own number. Students find the value of the sum on the gameboard and connect 2 dots that are adjacent to the number. If that line closes the square, they capture it and shade it in their color. The player to shade in 3 squares first is the winner.

## Activity 2

Revisit Five in a Row-Add within 100 with Composing

![Image](document_artifacts/image_000026_f013eb7f9888f915628068a647f5d3a98d57c697e8b7ad3c75b0a6943ebe54b0.png)

## Standards

Addressing 2.NBT.B.5

The purpose of this activity is for students to revisit Stage 6 of the Five in a Row center, which was introduced in IM Grade 1. In this stage, students add within 100 including sums that require composing a ten when adding by place. Partner A places a paper clip on the gameboard to cover one number in each gray row. They add the numbers and place their color counter on the sum. Partner B moves one of the paper clips to a different number in the same row, adds the numbers, and places their color counter on the sum. Students take turns until a player gets 5 counters in a row or the board is filled.

The gameboards will be used again in later lessons. Consider copying on cardstock or laminating for future use.

## Launch

- Groups of 2
- Give each group a gameboard, 2 paper clips, and two-color counters.
- 'We are going to learn a game called Five in a Row.'
- Display the gameboard.
- 'First I need to pick one number from each gray row to add together. I put a paper clip on one number in the top row and one number in the bottom row. Then I add them together.'
- Pick two numbers and add them.
- 'The value of my sum is \_\_\_\_\_. Now I look for the number on the gameboard and cover it with a counter. Then it is my partner's turn.'
- 'My partner moves one of the paper clips to another number in the same row. Then they must find the sum of the two numbers.'
- If needed, select a student volunteer to be Partner B to show the steps.
- 'Before you begin, you and your partner need to decide who will use the red side of the counters, and who will use the yellow side. Then take turns moving one paper clip, adding the numbers, and covering the sum. If you find a sum that is already covered, move the same paper clip to a different number. The game ends when one person gets five counters in a row or the gameboard is filled. The counters can be in a row across, up and down, or diagonal.'
