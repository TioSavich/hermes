The Pythagorean theorem is a fundamental principle in geometry, which states that in a right-angled triangle, the square of the length of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of the squares of the lengths of the other two sides. This can be written as:

\[ a^2 + b^2 = c^2 \]

where \( c \) represents the length of the hypotenuse, and \( a \) and \( b \) represent the lengths of the other two sides.
