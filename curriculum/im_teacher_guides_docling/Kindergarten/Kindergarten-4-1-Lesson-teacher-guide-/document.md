## Goals

- Comprehend (in spoken language) the phrase 'how many altogether?' refers to the total number of objects given 2 groups of objects.
- Determine the total number of objects, within 10, given 2 groups of objects, and express the total as a written number.

## Lesson Purpose

The purpose of this lesson is for students to count 2 groups of objects to find the total, within 10.

## Narrative

In a previous unit, students counted groups of up to 10 objects, recognized and wrote numbers 1-10, and connected numbers to quantities. In this lesson, students are introduced to addition as they count to find the total of 2 groups. Many students may approach these activities the same way that they answered 'how many?' questions in previous units-by putting the 2 groups of objects together and then counting all of the objects. The Activity Syntheses focus on different strategies for finding the total number of objects given 2 groups of objects.

The addition language ' add ,' 'adding,' and 'put together' is used throughout the lesson. Students are not expected to produce this language until later in the unit. The notation '\_\_\_\_\_ and \_\_\_\_\_' is introduced by the teacher in Activity 2 as one way to represent combining the 2 groups. Students need time to see and hear teachers using this notation and other addition language before they can understand the more abstract language of 'plus' or an expression '\_\_\_\_\_ + \_\_\_\_\_' which will be introduced in a later section. Putting together two groups of objects to determine the total prepares students for work in a future unit in which they will represent and solve Put Together, Total Unknown story problems.

## Launch

- Groups of 2
- Give each group of students a number mat and connecting cubes.
- 'We are going to learn a new center called Tower Build. You will each try to make a tower with 10 cubes. The first partner who has 10 cubes in their tower wins.'
- 'Take turns rolling a cube onto the mat to figure out how many cubes to add to your tower. After you add cubes to your tower, check to see if you or your partner have 10 cubes in your tower.'
- 'If you have more than 10 cubes in your tower, take off the extra cubes and use those cubes to start a new tower.'

## Activity

- 10 minutes: partner work time
- 'Now you can choose another center. You can also continue playing Tower Build.'
- Display the center choices in the student book.
- Invite students to work at the center of their choice.
- 10 minutes: center work time

## Activity Synthesis

- Display a tower of 6 cubes.
- Count the cubes in the tower or invite a student to count the cubes.
- 'Elena has a tower of 6 cubes. She rolled a 3.'
