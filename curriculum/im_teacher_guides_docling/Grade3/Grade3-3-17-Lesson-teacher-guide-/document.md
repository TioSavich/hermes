## Goals

- Explain (orally and in writing) how to determine if an estimate for a solution to a word problem is reasonable.
- Solve two-step word problems, using addition and subtraction.

## Lesson Purpose

The purpose of this lesson is for students to use mental computation and estimation strategies, such as rounding to decide if answers to two-step word problems make sense.

## Narrative

Previously, students extended their understanding of addition and subtraction within 1,000 and learned how to round to the nearest ten and the nearest hundred. In this lesson, students work with two-step word problems and decide if a given answer for a two-step problem is reasonable. Students estimate answers to two-step problems and determine if each other's solutions make sense after they solve two-step word problems in a way that makes sense to them.

## Student Task Statement

There are 124 beads in the bin before 96 more beads are added. Then 53 beads are used to make a bracelet.

Tyler says there are 273 beads in the bin now.

Explain why Tyler's statement doesn't make sense.

## Student Response

Sample response: Tyler's statement doesn't make sense because if about 100 beads are added to 124 beads, that's about 225 beads. Then about 50 beads are used to make a bracelet, which would take the number back down close to 175 beads. Using 50 beads to make a bracelet would make fewer beads in the bin, not more beads.

## Responding to Student Thinking

Students do not explain why Tyler's statement does not make sense.
