## Goals

- Explain (orally) strategies for solving problems that involve metric measurements, with whole numbers and multiplicative comparison.

## Lesson Purpose

The purpose of this lesson is for students to apply what they learned about metric units of measurement and multiplicative comparison to solve multi-step problems.

## Narrative

In the preceding lessons, students learned about the relationship between meters and centimeters, kilometers and meters, kilograms and grams, and liters and milliliters. In this lesson, students integrate what they learned to solve problems that are less straightforward and that require them to reason in multiple steps.

- MLR1 Stronger and Clearer Each Time

In this activity, students apply their knowledge of centimeters, meters, and kilometers, perform unit conversions, and reason multiplicatively to compare and order distances.

Students have the opportunity to decide which unit to use for making comparisons (that is, whether to convert all distances to meters, to centimeters, or to kilometers). They may find it most practical to use meters because two of the distances are already in meters, and because they know how the other two units are related to meters.

Students reason abstractly and quantitatively as they think about which unit to use to make comparisons (MP2), and use place-value understanding to make the conversions (MP7).

This activity uses MLR1 Stronger and Clearer Each Time. Advances: reading, writing.

Halfway through a soccer game, Han drank 210 mL of water. At the end of the game, he drank 4 times as much as he did at halftime.

Did Han drink more or less than 1 L of water in total? Explain or show your reasoning.

## Student Response

More than 1 L. Sample response: One liter is 1,000 mL. At the end of the game, Han drank or 840 mL. Combined with the 210 mL from halftime, that is or 1,050 mL. This is 50 mL more than 1 L.
