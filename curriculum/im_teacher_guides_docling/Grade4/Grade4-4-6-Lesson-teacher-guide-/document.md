## Goals

- Explain (orally) how to represent thousands and tenthousands using base-ten blocks or diagrams.
- Represent 5-digit numbers with base-ten blocks and diagrams.

## Lesson Purpose

The purpose of this lesson is to develop a relative sense of ten-thousand and understand it as a unit consisting of 10 units of one-thousand.

## Narrative

In this lesson, students build on their understanding of the base-ten structure to develop a sense of the magnitude of 10,000. They first use base-ten blocks and base-ten diagrams to build four-digit and five-digit numbers. They then use a 10-by-10 grid to represent 100 and work together to build a representation of 1,000, and then 10,000. Students may notice the inherent multiplicative structure of the 10-by-10 grids or the array of 10,000 and use counting strategies to identify significant groups of 10 (for example, 10 groups of 100 and 10 groups of 1,000).

## Lesson Synthesis

'Today we used base-ten blocks, grids, and drawings to represent multi-digit numbers. We also used groups of hundreds to build 10,000.'

'In first grade, we learned that 10 ones are in each unit of ten. In second grade, we learned that 10 tens are in each unit of one hundred. If we count 10 units of a hundred, we have a thousand, which is a new unit.'

'Where in this class chart do you see ten of something making a new unit?' (Ten of the hundred grids make a row or a unit of one thousand. Ten of the thousand rows make a unit of ten-thousand.)

'How might we represent a number like 13,000?' (Add three more rows of 1,000 to the chart.)

'What do you think the next unit will be after ten-thousands?' (hundred-thousands or millions)

'Ten groups of ten-thousand makes a new unit, hundred-thousand. We will learn about this unit in future lessons.'
