## Goals

- Determine (orally) if a story problem does not have enough information to solve the problem or if it has more than enough information to solve the problem.

## Lesson Purpose

The purpose of this lesson is for students to analyze story problems that give too much or too little information.

## Narrative

This lesson is optional because it does not address any new mathematical content standards. This lesson does provide students with an opportunity to apply precursor skills of mathematical modeling (MP4).

In previous lessons, students solved several different types of story problems. In this lesson, the story problems either have extra, unnecessary information or not enough information to solve the problem. Students group the story problems into one of these two categories. They then cross out the unnecessary information and solve the problems. Students also see that sometimes a story can have extra information and also not have all of the necessary information to solve the problem.

- Card Sort

The purpose of this activity is for students to think about the information needed to solve different types of story problems. In previous activities, students solved many different types of story problems given the necessary information. In this activity, students sort story problems into two categories: those that do not include enough information to solve the problem and those with some extra information given in the problem that is not needed to solve the problem. This sorting task gives students opportunities to analyze story problems closely and make connections (MP7). When students identify information they need to know and don't need to know to answer a question, they model with mathematics (MP4).
