## Goals

- Use tools to create a design that follows constraints about length.

## Lesson Purpose

The purpose of this lesson is for students to measure length and cut paper to a specified length.

## Narrative

This lesson is optional because it does not address any new mathematical content standards. This lesson does provide students with an opportunity to apply precursor skills of mathematical modeling (MP4).

In previous lessons, students measured the length of objects by iterating same-size length units without gaps or overlaps. They learned that the number of units they used is the length of the object. In this lesson, the class makes a class quilt out of rectangular pieces of paper that they design and create borders for. Students begin by making decisions about the size of the rectangles and drawing their own rectangle based on the decisions the class made. They then determine how to make a border for the rectangle that is one cube longer on each side than the rectangle itself. Students use any method that makes sense to them to make the border. Finally, all of the rectangles are put together into a class quilt.

## Activity

- Display images.
- 'Here are some examples of other quilts that might help us make our decisions. Do you think all of our patches should be the same size? Be ready to explain your thinking.'
- 2 minutes: partner discussion
- Share responses.
- If the class decides the patches should be the same size:
- 'How big do you think our patches should be? Use your cubes to show how long each side of the patch should be.' (12 cubes on the top and bottom and 12 cubes on the sides, 10 cubes on the top and bottom and 13 cubes on the sides)
- 3 minutes: partner work time
- Share responses and decide how big the patches will be.
- 'Let's draw the outline of our patches on paper. Use the cubes to make sure each side of your patch is the right length.'
- 4 minutes: independent work time
- If the class decided the patches do not have to be the same size:
- 'Is there a size for our patches that might be too small? Is there a size that might be too big? Use your cubes to show something that might be too small and another that might be too big.'
- 3 minutes: partner work time
- Share responses and decide on parameters such as, no fewer than 8 cubes on any side and no more than 15 cubes on any side.
- 'Now you can decide how big your patch will be and draw an outline of your patch on paper. Use the cubes to be sure each side isn't too long or too short.'
- 4 minutes: independent work time
- 'Now you are going to design your patch so that it shows something you are good at or something you feel proud of and want to share with the class.'
- Consider sharing your previously made patch as an example.
- 5 minutes: independent work time
