## Goals

- Ask (orally and in writing) questions to create story problems.
- Create equations that can be used to represent or solve story problems.
- Represent and solve a variety of story problems.

## Lesson Purpose

The purpose of this lesson is for students to represent and solve story problems that involve an unknown addend.

## Narrative

In previous lessons, students made sense of and matched equations to different story problems. In the last lesson, they wrote equations to match story problems or to show how they solved a story problem. In this lesson, students represent and solve story problems that can be represented as an equation with unknown addend. They are encouraged to write an equation with a symbol for the answer to the question, but they are not required to. Students may write a second equation using the relationship between addition and subtraction, the commutative property, or the meaning of the equal sign.

## Standards

Addressing

1.OA.A.1

The purpose of this activity is for students to make sense of story problems that do not include a question. The structure of the first story makes it likely most students will infer it is an Add To, Change Unknown problem without a question being asked. The actions and transitional words make it very likely all students will ask a question similar to 'How many more pictures did Clare cover?' However, the second story begins only by describing two different quantities. This could be the set-up to a Put Together/Take Apart problem or a Compare problem. This gives students an opportunity to make sense of different relationships between quantities and to revisit the different ways of asking to find the difference. When students formulate their own questions, they need to make sense of the given information in order to understand what is given and what is unknown (MP1).
