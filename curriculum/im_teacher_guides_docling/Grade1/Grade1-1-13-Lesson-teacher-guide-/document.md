## Goals

- Ask (orally) and answer questions that can be answered about data represented in a table.
- Explain (orally) how to determine if a question about data can be answered using a given representation.

## Lesson Purpose

The purpose of this lesson is for students to ask questions about data that can be answered by a given data representation.

## Narrative

In previous lessons students answered questions about data using different representations. In this lesson, students begin by determining whether or not questions can be answered by a given representation. Then, students think of questions that can be asked about the data. Finally, students answer one another's questions.

## Launch

- Groups of 2
- 'We are going to take another survey and ask questions about the data.'
- Decide on a survey question the class would like to answer and 3 possible responses. The survey question should be in the format 'Which [noun] is your favorite?'
- 'Record the title of the data representation and each category.'
- Demonstrate recording the titles. Students should do the same in their books.
- Divide the class into Group A and Group B. Each partnership should have one person from each group.
- 'Group A is going to take the survey first. If you are in Group B, record the data in your book as I record it for all to see.'
- Ask students in Group A the survey question and record the data in the Group A table using tally marks and then numbers.
- 'Now Group B is going to take the survey and Group A will record the data.'
- Ask students in Group B the survey question and record the data on the Group B table using tally marks and then numbers.
