## Goals

- Comprehend that percentages do not have to be a whole number.
- Recognize that 0.1% of a number is of 1% of the number.
- Use reasoning about place value to calculate percentages that are not whole numbers, and explain (orally) the strategy.

![Image](document_artifacts/image_000001_011de43459ad6f4ea54ae1b5c0d47e6c378cbbb8466e2c6a6d22e84ad0bb88ef.png)

## Learning Targets

- I can find fractional percentages (like 12.5% or 0.4%) of quantities.
- I understand that to find 0.1% of a quantity, I have to multiply by 0.001.

## Lesson Narrative

In this lesson, students consider how to calculate a fractional percentage using a whole number percentage as a reference. For example, if they know that 1% of 200 is 2, they can use the structure of the base-ten system to reason that 0.1% of 200 is 0.2, and 0.01% of 200 is 0.02 (MP7).

First, students use place value and properties of operations to calculate percentages without a context. Next, they apply this reasoning about fractional percentages to situations involving percent increase. This prepares students for working with more complex contexts, such as interest rates, taxes, tips, and measurement error.
