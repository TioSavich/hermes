# The fractal claim, measured — and the window instead of the shards

Date: 2026-07-25. Author: Claude (Opus 5, 1M context), working as this
repository's research assistant. Revised 2026-08-06 for readers arriving from
outside the project; every measurement stands as it stood on the original date,
when the corpus held 232 automata. Continues
`proposals/2026-07-25-mutual-noise-layers.md`. The artifact this report sized
was later built and tested; the two reports that did so are linked at the end.

## The setting

Hermes keeps a corpus of finite-state automata, each encoding one way a student
carries out arithmetic — productive strategies and their deformations side by
side — extracted from the mathematics-education literature and from the
Illustrative Mathematics K–8 curriculum. Every transition carries an action
from a canonical alphabet (122 actions at this writing), and every action
belongs to a register: constitution, partition, transformation, search,
comparison, iteration, operation, inscription, normative, delegation. The
question this report measures comes from the manuscript the corpus serves
(*Understanding Mathematics as an Emancipatory Discipline: A Critical Theory
Approach*): whether the corpus in fact has the shape the manuscript calls
fractal.

## A correction first

An earlier draft objected that polarity was a seam in the layered account of
student discourse this report continues: a denial is signal for the discursive
layer *and* for the mathematical one, where a scheme of mutually-noisy layers
would seem to predict it belongs to one. The objection had the structure
backwards. If the architecture is generated by determinate negation and the
determinate negation of determinate negation, then what recurs at every scale
is exactly negation. A layer scheme in which negation showed up in only one
layer would be the anomaly. Polarity being signal for both layers is the
self-similarity, not a leak in it.

## The claim is precise, and it is not a metaphor

The manuscript's Appendix B, in the section "Fractal Architecture: Iterative
Core + Strategic Shell":

> Across strategies I repeatedly recover the same **fractal pattern**:
> **Iterative Core:** A minimal loop (initialize → step (+1, −1, +Base, +Chunk) →
> condition check). … **Strategic Shell:** A supervisory layer that *prepares*,
> *optimizes*, or *transforms* the problem so that the core runs fewer or
> cognitively lighter iterations. … Because the shell often *invokes* the core as a
> subroutine … strategies *contain* (and sometimes nest) earlier strategies. This
> produces a genuine computational fractal — not metaphorical flourish, but
> recurrence of the same control schema at different conceptual scales.

That is a structural claim with two testable halves, and both are answerable
from the corpus as it stands. The manuscript's first chapter supplies a second
sense of the same claim — a melody repeating identically *relative to* a rising
key, "the underlying structure moves, while its representation stays the same,"
with the constancy of the tonic recalling the transcendental unity of
apperception.

### Half one: the shell invokes the core

The invocation exists in the corpus as the **delegation** register — the
actions `dispatch_to_kernel` and `receive_kernel_outcome`, by which one
automaton hands a subproblem to a subordinate loop and receives its outcome.

**14 of 232 automata carry a delegation edge.** Twelve of the fourteen are
fraction automata, which is where Appendix B locates the nesting (the
Fractional Composition Scheme calling the entire Partitive Fraction Scheme as a
subroutine):

| automaton | delegation edges |
|---|---|
| `fraction/splitting` | `receive_kernel_outcome` ×2 |
| `fraction/co_denominator_count_on_from_larger` | `dispatch_to_kernel`, `receive_kernel_outcome` |
| `fraction/co_denominator_make_base_transfer` | `dispatch_to_kernel`, `receive_kernel_outcome` |
| `fraction/cross_multiplication_rule_from_pattern` | `dispatch_to_kernel` |
| `fraction/recursive_partition`, `solve_for_unit`, `improper_fraction_iteration`, `unit_fraction_iteration`, `unit_fraction_partition`, `improper_fraction_chain_loss` | `receive_kernel_outcome` |
| `algebraic/programming_expression_evaluation`, `calculus/factor_cancel_substitute` | `receive_kernel_outcome` |
| `discourse/entitlement_by_deferral`, `discourse/deferral_regress` | `defer_to_asserter` + inherit / regress |

`fraction/splitting` is the strongest instance: two `receive_kernel_outcome`
edges, one from `partition_trace` and one from `iterate_trace`. A shell
receiving outcomes from two cores, which is the manuscript's structure exactly
— and the delegation edges were extracted from source material before this
claim was tested, not authored for it.

The two discourse rows deserve their own sentence. `defer_to_asserter` — the
discursive move of resting a claim on another speaker's authority — is recorded
as kin to `dispatch_to_kernel` in `action_kinship/3`, and the kinship row
predates this measurement. The same control schema recurs across genres as
well as within one. That is the "different conceptual scales" clause, and it is
a fact about the data rather than a reading of it.

### Half two: the shell/core shape without an explicit call

Most automata do not call a subroutine and still have the shape. Grouping the
registers into shell (constitution, partition, transformation, search,
comparison), core (iteration, operation) and closure (inscription, normative):

| | automata |
|---|---:|
| register word parses as shell-prefix → core → closure | **168 of 232 (72%)** |
| with both a shell and a core present | 85 |
| shell with no core (pure preparation or transformation) | 74 |
| core with no shell | 8 |
| does not parse in that order | 64 |

The manuscript's own worked example checks out. Sarah's 8 + 5, which Hackenberg
calls rearranging to make bases:

```
make_ten_split_leftover:
  assign_roles > decompose_operand > regroup_to_base > combine_quantities > record_conservation
  constitution   partition          transformation    operation             normative
  ├──────── shell: find the gap, split ────────┤├── core: make base, add leftover ──┤├ record ┤
```

Which is Sarah's "eight plus two is ten, but then two plus three is five … so
you take care of two, and you need to add three more."

So the fractal claim in its precise sense is measured rather than asserted: 72%
parse shell → core → closure, 14 explicit subroutine invocations concentrated
in the fraction family, and the schema recurring across two genres through a
kinship pair recorded independently.

**What that does not establish.** 64 automata of 232 do not parse in that
order, and this report did not diagnose whether they are a different schema or
a defect in the grouping. Sixty-four is 28%, which is too many to call residue.

## The window instead of the shards

The measurement above raises a design question: if 232 automata spell the same
small set of control shapes, a model consulting the corpus should not need the
raw corpus. The manuscript's author put the ask as an image — serve "the
stained glass window" rather than "ten million shards" — and the window became
the working name for a one-line-per-automaton index.

Measured, at roughly four characters to a token:

| what the raw material costs | ~tokens |
|---|---:|
| transition tables + the discursive genre | 99,400 |
| the vocabulary map | 112,300 |
| the grammar layer | 47,400 |
| the attested phrases | 62,700 |
| the corpus rows behind them | 405,000 |
| **all of it** | **~727,000** |

And the window — one line per automaton, its arc, its shell, its core:

```
addition/base_ones_chunking arc=work_then_keep shell=decompose_operand core=combine_quantities+combine_quantities
addition/column_addition_with_carrying arc=keep_work_keep_work_keep shell=align_to_common_unit+regroup_to_base core=apply_stored_rule+accumulate_total
addition/append_column_sum_without_carrying arc=keep_first_break_recover_break shell=align_to_common_unit core=substitute_operation
```

| | ~tokens |
|---|---:|
| 232 automaton lines | 7,500 |
| the legend: 122 actions with register and stance | 1,300 |
| the legend: 24 arc names | 130 |
| **window + legend** | **~8,900 estimated; 12,863 measured when built** |

**Estimated 81× smaller, and self-describing** — the legend is what a model
needs to read the window, and both fit inside it.

### What the window actually is

It is an index, not a replacement, and the difference matters. The window
drops which local label an edge carried, the citations, the evidence text, the
confidence, the corpus excerpt — everything the corpus has been careful to
keep.

Two corrections arrived when the index was built
(`2026-07-25-the-index-and-its-negation.md`). The window **preserves** step
order within each of shell, core and closure, and loses the *interleaving
between* them; an earlier version of this section had that backwards. And the
built artifact measures **12,863 tokens rather than the 8,900 estimated
here**, because the served form carries the closure and delegation lists and a
two-line layout meant for reading. The estimates in the table stand as first
written, marked, so the size of the error stays on the record: it was 44%
optimistic, and the direction of a compression estimate made before building
is worth knowing.

What the window preserves is enough to *choose*: given a question, a model can
read the window, name the automaton or the arc it wants, and then be served
that automaton's full record. The corpus is served whole at one scale, and one
pane at full depth. The compression is not a summary of the corpus; it is a
retrieval surface over it, and the reason it can be small is that the arc
layer already did the compressing — 232 automata spelling 24 arcs.

## Honest limits

- **Four characters to a token is an order-of-magnitude estimate**, not a
  measurement. A real tokenizer would move these numbers by tens of percent and
  not by factors.
- **The ratio was arithmetic when this was written, and has since been tested.**
  `2026-07-25-the-window-was-asked.md` ran the comparison this report called
  for: at identical token budget the index routed 9 of 30 cited phrases to
  their automaton against the raw slice's 1 (p = 0.0215). So the ratio now has
  one result behind it — on 30 items, one model, and with a filter that
  handicaps the keyword baseline, all stated there.
- **28% of automata do not fit the shell/core shape**, and this report did not
  diagnose them. The 72% figure is the claim; the 64 exceptions are the work.
- **The delegation count is 14 and the corpus is 232.** That the nesting
  concentrates in fraction matches the manuscript, and fourteen instances is a
  small base for "genuine computational fractal" as a claim about the corpus
  as a whole. It is a strong claim about the fraction schemes specifically.
- **The shell/core grouping is this report's own.** Which registers count as
  shell and which as core is a decision made to test the manuscript's claim,
  and a different grouping would move the 72%. The registers themselves were
  authored before this claim was in view, which is the only thing keeping the
  test from being circular.
- **Nothing was built here.** No file changed but this one. The window was
  computed to decide whether it was worth building; on this evidence it was.

## Result

- The polarity objection is withdrawn: negation recurring at every layer is
  the self-similarity, not an exception to it.
- The manuscript's fractal claim is structural and testable, and the corpus
  answers it directly.
- **14 of 232 automata invoke a subroutine**, 12 of them fraction, `splitting`
  invoking two; **168 of 232 (72%)** parse shell → core → closure; the schema
  crosses the two genres through a kinship pair recorded before the claim was
  tested.
- **~727,000 tokens of shards against ~8,900 of window and legend as estimated
  here, 81×** — and the window is a retrieval surface rather than a summary,
  which is what makes the number honest.
